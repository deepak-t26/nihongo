LIMS Fundamentals

The domain model the guide expects you to memorize and explain.

What is a LIMS?

A Laboratory Information Management System manages samples, tests, results and associated laboratory data across the lifecycle from registration through approval and reporting, while enforcing workflows, specifications and controls such as audit trails, e-signatures and access control. The guide frames pharma LIMS as the system of record for QC results supporting batch release.

10-step sample lifecycle

1 Registration→2 Receipt→3 Test assignment→4 Testing/result entry→5 Spec check→6 Review→7 Approval→8 Reporting→9 Release/disposition→10 Archive

Master/static data

Products & tests

Products/materials, specifications, test methods, components, calculations, units, rounding and limits.

Operations

Sampling plans, sample types, lots/batches, storage locations, workflows and report templates.

Resources

Instruments, calibration/maintenance status, reagents and standards with expiry.

People & controls

Users, roles, privileges, training records and controlled lifecycle states.

QC concepts

OOS — Out of Specification

Result outside specification. The guide describes Phase I laboratory investigation followed, when needed, by Phase II broader investigation, with controlled retesting/resampling. It explicitly warns against “testing into compliance”.

OOT — Out of Trend

Within specification but abnormal compared with historical data; especially important in stability work.

Deviation / CAPA

Deviation is departure from an approved procedure. CAPA is corrective and preventive action.

Change control

Formal assessment, approval, implementation and verification of changes, including LIMS configuration.

The guide positions a typical LIMS as Category 4, with custom scripts/interfaces treated as Category 5, and says validation effort should be scaled to risk to patient safety, product quality and data integrity.

CSV vs CSA

CSV

Traditional computer system validation with heavier, document-driven scripted testing.

CSA

Risk- and critical-thinking-oriented assurance: deeper testing for high-risk functions and efficient evidence/testing for lower-risk functions.

Troubleshooting — 8-step method

  1. Understand and reproduce: who/what/when/environment/recent changes.
  2. Assess impact and urgency, especially GxP and batch-release impact.
  3. Collect evidence: application/DB/audit/interface/OS logs.
  4. Isolate layer-by-layer and compare working vs failing cases.
  5. Form and test a hypothesis in a test environment, one variable at a time.
  6. Apply workaround, then permanent fix through change control and regression testing.
  7. Identify root cause and prevention using 5 Whys/fishbone.
  8. Close with a written customer incident report and lessons learned.

Final Checklists

Use these as the final readiness dashboard.

Knowledge self-check

One week before

  • Research target company/products/customers/news.
  • Review regulation cheat sheet and model answers.
  • Run two mock interviews, including one in Japanese.
  • Prepare Mini-LIMS demo and one-page portfolio summary.
  • Prepare English CV and Japanese 履歴書 / 職務経歴書 if requested.

Interview day

  • Test camera, microphone, background and Japanese IME.
  • Open politely: 「本日はお時間をいただき、ありがとうございます。どうぞよろしくお願いいたします。」
  • Answer conclusion-first (結論から), then reason, then example.
  • If you do not know something, be honest and explain how you would find out.
  • Close with thanks, two questions and a short same-day thank-you email.
